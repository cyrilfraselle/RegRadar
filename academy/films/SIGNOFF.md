## Film 02 · Effort follows risk (`aml-basics`, 58 s)

**Premise.** Brindle Pay, a fictional payment and e-money firm, takes its prepaid card to a new country. The film shows the firm assessing its own risks before launch, sizing each customer's checks to the risk, and recording why. Risks beat: a pattern commonly linked to money mules (warning sign, not proof).

**Five key legal claims**

1. The business-wide risk assessment is drawn up by the compliance officer and approved by the management body in its management function; it is documented, kept up to date, regularly reviewed and available to supervisors on request. (AMLR Art. 10(2); Art. 10(3) (no waiver for financial institutions))
2. Before a new product, or an existing product in a new geographical area, the related risks are identified and assessed and measures taken. (AMLR Art. 10(1), second subparagraph)
3. The extent of due diligence rests on an individual risk analysis taking the business-wide assessment into account; lower risk: may simplify; increased risk: enhanced measures apply. (AMLR Art. 20(2))
4. Enhanced measures may include source of funds and wealth, senior management approval and enhanced monitoring; under simplified measures, monitoring must still be sufficient to detect unusual or suspicious transactions. (AMLR Art. 34(4)(c), (e), (f); Art. 33(1), second subparagraph)
5. The firm must at all times be able to demonstrate that its measures are appropriate to the risks; controls not commensurate with the risks can lead to administrative measures, and serious, repeated or systematic breaches to fines. (AMLR Art. 20(4), 21(3); AMLD6 Art. 56(1)(c), 55(1))

**Still disputable or worth your eye**

- Row 9: the "four habits" are a teaching model, not law. They are now shown as not a sequence; the REPORT card pulses only when a suspicion is triggered.
- Brindle Pay's status as a financial institution rests on CRD Annex I points 4 and 15 via AMLR Art. 2(1)(6)(a). The Annex text is not in the repo; PSD2 Art. 113 and EMD2 Art. 20, which are in the repo, corroborate it.

## Film 03 · Know who, know why (`cdd-kyc`, 60 s)

**Premise.** A rushed onboarding: Larkmoor Trading, a fictional company set up last month, wants to send €48,000 today through Brindle Pay. The film covers when CDD applies, that it is a set of measures, that the account may open but nothing moves until the customer and the beneficial owners are identified and verified, and what happens when Larkmoor will not name its owners: the account is closed and a report considered.

**Final captions** (the exact words on screen; still captions are in the ledger)

| | Time | Caption |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | 0:00 to 0:06 | One example scenario: Larkmoor Trading, a fictional company set up last month, asks Brindle Pay, a fictional payment and e-money firm, for a business account and wants to send €48,000 today. The situation: a rushed onboarding. |
| C2 | 0:06 to 0:13 | Due diligence applies when a business relationship starts, as here. It also applies to occasional transactions from EUR 10 000, lower for some firms, to any suspicion whatever the amount, and to doubts about the data or about who you are dealing with. |
| C3 | 0:13 to 0:21 | It is a set of measures, applied together: identify the customer and verify its identity, identify the beneficial owners, take reasonable measures to verify them and understand the ownership and control structure, check sanctions and politically exposed persons, and identify and verify whoever acts for the company and confirm they are authorised. |
| C4 | 0:21 to 0:27 | Then understand the business: what Larkmoor does, why it wants the account and, where needed, how much it expects to move, where the money comes from and where it goes. |

| C6 | 0:34 to 0:42 | In this example, Larkmoor will not name the people behind its holding company. It records the decision and why. |
| C7 | 0:42 to 0:48 | For customers who are taken on, due diligence does not stop at onboarding. Information is updated at least every year for higher-risk customers, at least every five years for others, and when circumstances change. |
| C8 | 0:48 to 0:56 | Common warning signs, not proof: questions avoided, documents that do not agree, a structure with no business reason, pressure to move money before checks are done. One way it goes wrong: onboard now, collect later, never close the gap. Serious, repeated or systematic breaches can be fined. |
| C9 | 0:56 to 1:00 | Know who, know why, and keep knowing. The AMLR, Regulation (EU) 2024/1624, applies from 10 July 2027. |

**Five key legal claims**

1. CDD applies when a business relationship starts, for occasional transactions from EUR 10 000 (lower for some firms, e.g. EUR 1 000 for occasional transfers of funds by financial institutions), on any suspicion whatever the amount, and on doubts about data or identity. (AMLR Art. 19(1)(a), (b), (d), (e), (f); 19(2), 19(3))
2. All CDD measures apply: identify and verify the customer; identify the beneficial owners, take reasonable measures to verify them and understand the structure; sanctions and PEP checks; identify and verify anyone acting for the customer and confirm authority; purpose and nature of the business. (AMLR Art. 20(1)(a) to (i); Art. 25)
3. Verification comes before the relationship (limited lower-risk exceptions); a credit or financial institution may open the account only with safeguards so that no transactions run until Art. 20(1)(a) and (b) are fully complied with. (AMLR Art. 23(1), 23(2), 23(3))
4. If CDD cannot be completed: refrain from the transaction or relationship, terminate an existing one (here, close the opened account), consider a report to the FIU, and record the decision and its reasons. (AMLR Art. 21(1), 21(3); Art. 69(1), second subparagraph)
5. Customer information is updated at least every year for customers under enhanced measures and at least every five years for others, and when circumstances change. (AMLR Art. 26(2), 26(3)(a))

**Still disputable or worth your eye**

- Row 17: the caption names identification and verification of the customer and the beneficial owners as the condition for transactions. Art. 20(1)(b) also requires understanding the ownership and control structure; that is stated in C3 but not repeated in C5.
- Row 22: the headline "Cannot finish? Do not proceed." is a slogan. The carve-outs in Art. 21(1), second to fourth subparagraphs, and Art. 21(2) are not shown. The reviewer marked v1 "No file, no deal." as disputable.
- Not taken: the reviewer's optional addition "and no other source shows them" to row 19. It would point towards the Art. 22(2) route (no beneficial owner identified) instead of Art. 21.
- Brindle Pay's status: same note as film 02.
- Caption density is about 6.2 words a second (same as film 01).

## Film 04 · Look through (`beneficial-ownership`, 60 s)

**Premise.** Velmont Analytics, a fictional company, applies to Orrin, a fictional crypto-asset service provider. A stake is split so that no piece looks big. Mira holds 30 % directly. Jonas holds 8 % directly plus 45 % × 40 % = 18 % through Kappa Holding, so 26 % in total. Victor holds no shares but can appoint or remove a majority of the board. Elif (14 %) and Tom (8 %) are not beneficial owners on these facts. On screen: no one controls Kappa Holding, so Art. 54 does not apply.

**Final captions** (the exact words on screen; still captions are in the ledger)

| | Time | Caption |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | 0:00 to 0:06 | One example scenario: Velmont Analytics, a fictional company, applies for an account at Orrin, a fictional crypto-asset service provider. The pattern to watch, one common example: a stake split up so that no single piece looks big. A warning sign, not proof. |
| C2 | 0:06 to 0:12 | A beneficial owner is any natural person who ultimately owns or controls a legal entity. Control by other means is checked in parallel with ownership. |
| C3 | 0:12 to 0:18 | Ownership means 25 % or more of the shares, voting rights or other ownership interest, directly or indirectly. Mira holds 30 % directly: she is a beneficial owner. |
| C4 | 0:18 to 0:26 | Through a company, multiply along the chain. Kappa Holding owns 40 % of Velmont, and Jonas owns 45 % of Kappa: 45 % of 40 % is 18 %. Elif reaches 14 %, Tom 8 %. |
| C5 | 0:26 to 0:32 | Then add his direct and indirect holdings together. Jonas also holds 8 % of Velmont directly: 18 % plus 8 % is 26 %. He is a beneficial owner. |
| C6 | 0:32 to 0:39 | Victor holds no shares, but an agreement gives him the right to appoint or remove a majority of the board. That is control by other means: he is a beneficial owner too. On these facts, Elif and Tom are not. |
| C7 | 0:39 to 0:46 | Identify all three, take reasonable measures to verify who they are, and consult the central register as well. Differences from the register are, as a rule, reported to it. |
| C8 | 0:46 to 0:53 | Common warning signs, not proof: nominees with no explanation, long or circular chains with no business reason, an owner who will not be named. Miss an owner and you miss the sanctions and politically exposed person checks on that person. |
| C9 | 0:53 to 0:57 | If no one can be identified after exhausting all possible means, record that, and identify and verify the senior managing officials. If due diligence still cannot be completed, do not proceed. |
| C10 | 0:57 to 1:00 | Look through every layer to the natural persons behind it. The AMLR, Regulation (EU) 2024/1624, applies from 10 July 2027. |

**Five key legal claims**

1. A beneficial owner is any natural person who ultimately owns or controls a legal entity; control by other means is identified in parallel with ownership. (AMLR Art. 2(1)(28); Art. 51, second paragraph)
2. Ownership interest = 25 % or more, direct or indirect; indirect ownership multiplies along the chain unless Art. 54 applies; all shareholdings on every level are taken into account, so direct and indirect holdings of the same person are added. (AMLR Art. 52(1), first and second subparagraphs; Art. 53(2)(c), Art. 54; recital 108 as further reading)
3. The right to appoint or remove a majority of the board is control via other means, in any case; agreements can be the source. (AMLR Art. 53(3)(b), 53(4)(a))
4. Identify the beneficial owners, take reasonable measures to verify them, consult the central register in addition, and as a rule report discrepancies to it. (AMLR Art. 20(1)(b); Art. 22(7), last subparagraph; Art. 24(1) to (3))
5. If no natural person is identified after exhausting all possible means: record that, identify and verify the senior managing officials; if CDD still cannot be completed, do not proceed. (AMLR Art. 22(2); Art. 21(1))

**Still disputable or worth your eye**

- Row 11: adding the direct 8 % to the indirect 18 % rests on Art. 52(1), second subparagraph ("all shareholdings on every level of ownership"). Recital 108 says it outright but is not in the repo JSON; it is named as further reading. The reviewer agreed (verdict ok).
- Row 10: the on-screen line says "no one holds over 50 %", a shorthand for the text's "50 % plus one" (Art. 53(2)(c)).
- Caption density is about 5.3 words a second.
