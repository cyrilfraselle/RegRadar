## Storyboard

Same world, palette and camera grammar as films 01 to 05 (`academy/films/README.md`), always a dark
film, captions in the DOM, sound off by default, reduced-motion stills. Central object: **the title
card**, a customer card with a small crest that can be passed to a relative and that fades but does
not vanish after the person leaves office.

### S1 · Hook · 0:00 to 0:06
- **Visual**: a new-customer card `Customer V · fictional` arrives at the Brindle Pay desk; behind it a faint second card `Minister · Member State (fictional)` joined by a thin line labelled `sibling`.
- **Kinetic type**: `Who is` / `behind the title?`
- **Caption C1**: One example scenario: Customer V, a fictional person, applies to Brindle Pay, a fictional payment and e-money firm. Customer V is the sibling of a minister in a Member State. The situation: a customer with a link to a prominent public function.

### S2 · Who counts · 0:06 to 0:16
- **Visual**: three tiles slide in: `Entrusted with a prominent public function` (heads of State and government, ministers, members of parliament, supreme and constitutional court members, ambassadors, high-ranking officers shown as small icons) · `Family member` · `Close associate`. A strip: `Is or has been`.
- **Kinetic type**: `01 · WHO` / `Is, or has been.`
- **Caption C2**: A politically exposed person is a natural person who is or has been entrusted with a prominent public function, such as head of State, minister, member of parliament or ambassador, in a Member State, an international organisation, the Union or a third country. The measures also apply to family members and to persons known to be close associates. In this example, a sibling counts as a family member because the function is that of a minister.

### S3 · Find them · 0:16 to 0:26
- **Visual**: the onboarding checklist from film 03 with one line lit: `Is the customer, or a beneficial owner, a PEP, a family member or a close associate?` Beside it a book icon `National list of exact functions` and a single stacked list `Single list · Official Journal`.
- **Kinetic type**: `02 · FIND` / `Ask. Check. Record.`
- **Caption C3**: Part of customer due diligence is determining whether the customer, the beneficial owner and, where relevant, the person on whose behalf a transaction is carried out, is a politically exposed person, a family member or a close associate. To help, each Member State lists the exact functions that count nationally, and the Commission publishes a single list in the Official Journal.

### S4 · Three measures · 0:26 to 0:38
- **Visual**: three stamps land on Customer V's card in turn: `Senior management approval` · `Source of wealth and source of funds` · `Enhanced, ongoing monitoring`. A banner over all three: `On top of normal due diligence`.
- **Kinetic type**: `03 · THREE` / `On top of the usual checks.`
- **Caption C4**: On top of normal due diligence, the firm obtains senior management approval to carry out the transaction or to establish or continue the relationship, takes adequate measures to establish the source of wealth and the source of funds, and conducts enhanced, ongoing monitoring of the relationship. In this example the three are shown in turn; in practice they are applied together.

### S5 · After office · 0:38 to 0:47
- **Visual**: the crest on Customer V's relative fades but stays at a low glow; a calendar strip `12 months` appears under a bar `at least`, and the bar continues beyond it with a question mark `until the risk no longer exists`.
- **Kinetic type**: `04 · AFTER OFFICE` / `The risk does not leave with the job.`
- **Caption C5**: When a politically exposed person leaves the function, the firm takes the continuing risk into account and applies one or more enhanced measures until the risk no longer exists, and in any case for not less than 12 months after the person ceased to be entrusted with the function.

### S6 · What goes wrong · 0:47 to 0:55
- **Visual**: warm chips under `ONE WAY IT GOES WRONG`: `No check` · `Approval after the fact` · `Wealth never explained`. Under them a card with `Stated income` and `Funds arriving` that do not match, with a tag `A warning sign, not proof`. A thin rule: `Serious, repeated or systematic breaches → fines`.
- **Kinetic type**: `WHAT GOES WRONG` / `No question. No answer.`
- **Caption C6**: Common ways it goes wrong: nobody asks, approval comes after the account is open, or the source of wealth is accepted without being checked. One example of a warning sign: funds that do not fit the income the customer stated. A warning sign to examine, not proof. Member States must ensure fines for serious, repeated or systematic breaches of the due diligence rules.

### S7 · End card · 0:55 to 1:00
- **Kinetic type**: `A risk, not a ban. Ask. Approve. Watch.` RegRadar mark. Footers: `AMLR · Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 · applies from 10 July 2027` and `Fictional example. Not legal advice.`
- **Caption C7**: A politically exposed person is a risk to manage, not a customer to refuse by default: ask, get approval, understand the money, and keep watching. The AMLR, Regulation (EU) 2024/1624, applies from 10 July 2027.

## Open points for the independent check and the owner

1. Row 3: the definition says "prominent public functions" (plural); is the singular acceptable?
2. Row 4: examples are a selection of point (34)(a); is "such as" enough to avoid reading the list as closed?
3. Row 6: the sibling rule is limited to certain functions. Keep as a worked detail or drop to avoid over-teaching?
4. Row 16: no ban on PEP relationships appears in the stored Arts 42 to 46. Confirm that nothing else in the AMLR (for example the high-risk third-country provisions, Art. 29 and following) changes the message "a risk, not a ban" for the example shown.
5. Row 19: confirm that Art. 42, 45 and 46 sit in Chapter III as AMLD6 Art. 55(1)(b) requires.
6. The AMLA guidelines on close associates and risk levels (Art. 42(2), due 10 July 2027) and the lists under Art. 43: whether they exist is not in this repo, so the film does not say they do.
