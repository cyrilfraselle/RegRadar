# Film 03 · Know who, know why (module `cdd-kyc`)

Script v1, 2026-10-18. **Phase 1 of the script review gate** (`academy/SCRIPT_REVIEW.md`): brief,
storyboard and claim ledger. Nothing is built until the independent check (step 3) and owner
sign-off (step 5) are done.

Status: self-checked by the author against `docs/data/laws/32024R1624_EN.json` (AMLR) and
`docs/data/laws/32024L1640_EN.json` (AMLD6). Independent check: **pending**. Owner sign-off: **pending**.

## Storyboard

Same world, palette and camera grammar as film 01 (see `academy/films/README.md`). The film's
central object is **the customer file**: a tall card that builds itself block by block. Warm
blocks are open questions, cool blocks are checked. A "Send €48,000" button sits on the file the
whole time, locked until the file allows it. HUD: `FICTIONAL EXAMPLE` over `Larkmoor Trading`.

### S1 · Hook · 0:00 to 0:06

- **Camera**: fade from black; tight on a chat bubble (z 1.8), pull out to z 1.2 to reveal the
  empty file behind it.
- **Visual**: an onboarding form slides in, half filled. A chat bubble types on:
  `Can we send €48,000 today?` (fictional). The `Send €48,000` button appears on the file with a
  small padlock, warm.
- **Kinetic type**: `Before money moves,` / `know who is moving it.`
- **Caption C1**: One example scenario: Larkmoor Trading, a fictional company set up last month, asks Brindle Pay, a fictional payment firm, for a business account and wants to send €48,000 today. The situation: a rushed onboarding.
- **Sound**: pulse; a soft notification blip for the chat bubble.

### S2 · When · 0:06 to 0:13

- **Camera**: slides left to a column of four trigger tiles, z 1.0.
- **Visual**: four tiles in mono, the first lights cool with a tick `applies here`:
  1. `A business relationship starts`
  2. `Occasional transaction from EUR 10 000 · single or linked · lower for some firms`
  3. `Any suspicion · whatever the amount`
  4. `Doubts about the data, or about who you are dealing with`

  A small mono footnote under tile 2: `e.g. EUR 1 000 for some firms and transactions · Art. 19(2), (3)`.
- **Kinetic type**: header `01 · WHEN`, headline `When it applies.`
- **Caption C2**: Due diligence applies when a business relationship starts, as here. It also applies to occasional transactions from EUR 10 000, lower for some firms, to any suspicion whatever the amount, and to doubts about the data or about who you are dealing with.
- **Sound**: one tock per tile.

### S3 · What · 0:13 to 0:27

- **Camera**: back to the file, slow push in (z 1.0 → 1.15) as it grows.
- **Visual (0:13 to 0:21)**: eyebrow on the file: `ALL OF THE FOLLOWING`. Blocks stack up,
  warm first, each cooling with a tick as a comet of data flies in from a source icon (ID
  document, company register extract, sanctions list, PEP list, mandate):
  `Identify and verify the customer` · `Identify the beneficial owners · understand the structure` ·
  `Sanctions check` · `Politically exposed person check` · `Who acts for the company · authorised?`
  The beneficial owner block **stays warm**: a small `?` hangs on it (it pays off in S5).
- **Visual (0:21 to 0:27)**: a profile card unfolds from the side of the file, fields typing on:
  `Business: phone accessories wholesale` · `Why this account: pay suppliers, receive from retailers` ·
  `Expected: about €60,000 a month` · `Money from: retailers in the EU` · `Money to: suppliers abroad`
  (all fictional). A last block lands on the file: `Ongoing monitoring`, with a loop arrow.
- **Kinetic type**: header `02 · WHAT`, headline `A set of measures, not one check.`
- **Caption C3** (0:13 to 0:21): It is a set of measures, applied together: identify the customer and verify its identity, identify the beneficial owners and understand the ownership and control structure, check sanctions and politically exposed persons, and confirm that whoever acts for the company is authorised.
- **Caption C4** (0:21 to 0:27): Then understand the business: what Larkmoor does, why it wants the account and, where needed, how much it expects to move, where the money comes from and where it goes.
- **Sound**: tick per block; light tone as each block cools.

### S4 · Before money moves · 0:27 to 0:34

- **Camera**: pull back to show the dial from film 02 at the top of the file (continuity), z 1.0.
- **Visual**: the dial needle settles right of centre (Brindle Pay rates the rushed request
  higher, labelled `this example`). The file door swings open (`Account open`), but the
  `Send €48,000` button stays locked; a mono label slides under it: `no transactions until the
  customer and its beneficial owners are checked`.
- **Kinetic type**: header `03 · BEFORE MONEY MOVES`, headline `Open is not the same as live.`
- **Caption C5**: How deep you go follows the risk. Verification comes before the relationship starts, with limited exceptions for lower risk. A bank or other financial institution may open the account, but no transactions run until the customer and its beneficial owners have been checked.
- **Sound**: door click; padlock rattle (soft) when the button stays locked.

### S5 · If you cannot finish · 0:34 to 0:42

- **Camera**: push in on the warm beneficial owner block (z 1.0 → 1.6).
- **Visual**: the block's `?` grows into a mono line: `Owner: "a holding company abroad". Names: refused.`
  (fictional). The block cannot cool. The file then splits into three outcome cards that drop in
  sequence: `Do not open · do not run the transaction` · `End any existing relationship` ·
  `Consider a report to the FIU`. A fourth thin strip types under them: `Record the decision and why`.
- **Kinetic type**: header `04 · IF YOU CANNOT FINISH` (accent warm), headline `No file, no deal.`
- **Caption C6**: In this example, Larkmoor will not name the people behind its holding company. When due diligence cannot be completed, the firm does not open the relationship or run the transaction, ends any existing one, and considers a report to the Financial Intelligence Unit. It records the decision and why.
- **Sound**: low tone at "refused"; three tocks for the outcome cards.

### S6 · Keep it current · 0:42 to 0:48

- **Camera**: wide (z 0.85); Larkmoor's file slides out of frame left, greyed. Three other
  customer files (fictional, unnamed) slide in on a horizontal timeline.
- **Visual**: a ruler runs under the files with two markers: `higher-risk customers: at least every
  year` and `all others: at least every 5 years`. Small cool pulses appear on the ruler where
  `something changed` (a new address, a new owner), each triggering a refresh of that file.
- **Kinetic type**: header `05 · NOT ONE-OFF`, headline `Keep knowing.`
- **Caption C7**: For customers who are taken on, due diligence does not stop at onboarding. Information is updated at least every year for higher-risk customers, at least every five years for others, and when circumstances change.
- **Sound**: soft ticks on the ruler; light tone on each refresh.

### S7 · What goes wrong · 0:48 to 0:56

- **Camera**: back to centre, z 1.0, the files dimmed.
- **Visual (0:48 to 0:52)**: warm chips slide in under `COMMON WARNING SIGNS · NOT PROOF`:
  `Questions avoided` · `Documents that do not agree` · `Structure with no business reason` ·
  `Pressure to move money before checks are done`. The last chip gets the tick `this example`.
- **Visual (0:52 to 0:56)**: a short loop: a file marked `open now, collect later` gets a warm gap
  that never closes while transactions (small comets) flow through it. Under a thin rule:
  `Serious, repeated or systematic breaches → fines`.
- **Kinetic type**: header `WHAT GOES WRONG` (accent warm), headline `The gap that never closes.`
- **Caption C8**: Common warning signs, not proof: questions avoided, documents that do not agree, a structure with no business reason, pressure to move money before checks are done. One way it goes wrong: onboard now, collect later, never close the gap. Serious, repeated or systematic breaches can be fined.
- **Sound**: low tone at the chips; rapid ticks of comets through the gap.

### S8 · End card · 0:56 to 1:00

- **Kinetic type**: `Know who. Know why.` / `Keep knowing.` (`Keep knowing.` in cool). RegRadar
  mark. Mono footers: `AMLR · Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 · applies from 10 July 2027` and
  `Fictional example. Not legal advice.`
- **Caption C9**: Know who, know why, and keep knowing. The AMLR, Regulation (EU) 2024/1624, applies from 10 July 2027.
- **Sound**: end chord.

### Not verified against a primary source held in the repo

- Whether payment and e-money institutions are "financial institutions" under AMLR Art. 2(1),
  point (6)(a) (depends on Annex I to Directive 2013/36/EU, not in `docs/data/laws/`). Rows 6 and
  17 are worded so that the film does not depend on it, but the scenario assumes it.
- The warning signs (row 27) are practice wording, not taken from a primary source.
