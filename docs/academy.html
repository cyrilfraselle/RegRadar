Why an institution would buy this
A completion certificate proves attendance. This proves competence.
Supervisors have moved from asking whether training happened to asking whether it worked. FinCEN's April 2026 proposed rule shifts AML programme assessment toward effectiveness, and OCC Bulletin 2025-37 lets examiners carry forward a satisfactory conclusion on the training pillar rather than re-examining it — turning a documented training record into fewer examiner weeks. Neither is answerable with an attendance log.
The delivery mechanism is short, repeated practice rather than an annual course, because that is what the forgetting curve requires: retention falls below 30% within a fortnight of a single session. Each shift mixes new cases with review of material you have seen before, weighted toward whatever you keep getting wrong.

How it teaches
Three rules the whole thing follows
01
Consequence before explanation
You act first and find out afterwards. The explanation lands on a decision you already made and already care about, which is the difference between reading a red flag and recognising one.
02
The law arrives when it bites
Every outcome names the article behind the control that caught you. AMLR Art. 36 means nothing in a slide deck and a great deal when it is the reason your correspondent chain just collapsed.
03
Both failure modes hurt
Missing a case is scored, and so is drowning the investigations team. Real judgment lives in the tension between the two, and any training that only punishes one teaches the wrong instinct.
