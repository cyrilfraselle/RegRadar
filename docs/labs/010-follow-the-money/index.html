Follow the money

Money laundering is a journey in three stages. Every stage leaves a trail, and a compliance team can learn to see it.

Transcript

  1. 0:00 to 0:04

    A sum of dirty money

    €480,000 in criminal cash needs a way into the financial system. Its way in is Café Aurore, a fictional, cash-heavy business.

  2. 0:04 to 0:10

    Placement

    The cash is split into small deposits and paid into many accounts, often by several people, so no single payment stands out. This is called smurfing.

  3. 0:10 to 0:19

    Layering

    The money hops between companies and countries, dressed up as invoices, loans and trade payments, until the trail is tangled. Each company is owned by another one. Behind the shells, the beneficial owner, the person who ultimately owns or controls the company, stays hidden.

  4. 0:19 to 0:25

    Integration

    The money comes back looking legitimate: an apartment, a motor yacht, a healthy business balance.

  5. 0:25 to 0:29

    Where a compliance team catches it

    Customer due diligence asks who pays in and where the cash comes from. Transaction monitoring spots the splits and the hops. A suspicious transaction report takes what you found to the Financial Intelligence Unit.

  6. 0:29 to 0:30

    Every stage leaves a trail

    In the EU, these duties sit in the AMLR, Regulation (EU) 2024/1624, which applies from 10 July 2027.

Fictional example. Not legal advice. Café Aurore and every company, account, person and amount shown are invented.

The three stages are a teaching model. Real cases often skip a stage, run stages at the same time, or start with money that is already in a bank.
