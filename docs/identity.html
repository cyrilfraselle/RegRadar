RegRadar · Visual system v1

Regulation is a network you navigate.

Chapters are lines. Articles are stops. Cross-references are interchanges. Compliance work is wayfinding through a legal network — so the system that organises it borrows its discipline from signage, not from software marketing.

AMLR · Chapter III — Customer Due Diligence
Solid stop = obligation modelled with applicability and delta. Hollow stop = article parsed, judgment layer still to encode. 90° and 45° only; stops evenly spaced regardless of article length.
01 Why this system

Semantics before form

The subject is the statute book: numbered provisions, effective dates, obligation language. The design organises that material; it does not dress it up.

“Design without semantics is shallow.”

Trust is the product

A compliance officer acts on what they can verify. Hairline rules and hard rectangles read as record; gradients and soft shadows read as marketing.

“In a world where everybody screams, silence is noticeable.”

Refine, don't replace

The square “R” flag stays. A mark that is already in use is collective memory — it gets sharpened, not swapped for novelty.

“I love systems and despise happenstance.”

04 Signage components
Modality as a sign

05 The rules we keep
  • 01Two sizes per surface. Heading ≈ 2× body. Differentiate with weight, space and alignment — not new sizes or novelty faces.
  • 02Flush left, never justified. Centred text only for lapidary lines.
  • 03Type hangs from a rule. 2px major, 1px minor. The rule is structure, not ornament.
  • 04Radius 0. A record is a rectangle. No shadows, no gradients.
  • 05Colour is an identifier. One signal blue for the system; signal colours only where they carry meaning.
  • 06Measure, not full width. Body text stops at ~62 characters. Reading law is hard enough.
  • 07Motion only on arrival. 140 ms, one easing curve, and nothing that moves twice.
  • 08If you can see the layout, it is a bad layout.
